How to Calculate CGPA (With a Worked Example)

Published 2026-10-03

CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) is the single number most universities put on your transcript. Despite looking intimidating, the math behind it is simple multiplication and division. Here's exactly how it works.

What CGPA actually means

Your CGPA is the weighted average of every semester GPA you've earned. "Weighted" means semesters with more credit hours influence the result more than lighter semesters.

The formula

CGPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total Credit Hours

Quality points for one semester = Semester GPA × Credit Hours that semester.

Step-by-step example

Semester 1: GPA 3.50, 18 credit hours → 3.50 × 18 = 63.0 quality points

Semester 2: GPA 3.20, 15 credit hours → 3.20 × 15 = 48.0 quality points

CGPA = (63.0 + 48.0) ÷ (18 + 15) = 111 ÷ 33 = 3.36

Common mistakes

  • Averaging the GPAs directly — (3.50 + 3.20) ÷ 2 = 3.35 looks close here, but with unequal credit hours it gives the wrong answer.
  • Forgetting retakes — universities handle repeated courses differently; check whether both attempts or only the latest counts.

Quick answers

What is CGPA?

CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) is the weighted average of all your semester GPAs, weighted by credit hours.

What is the CGPA formula?

CGPA equals total quality points divided by total credit hours, where quality points = semester GPA × credit hours.
